Standards

How We Check

The same standard the Hive’s case files use: public records only, linked where they’re used, and nothing about a named person that a record doesn’t support.

Page published Sep 25, 2026 · Last updated Sep 25, 2026

IN PLAIN WORDSWe leave opinions alone. We check facts. Confirmed facts get a link. Facts about a person or group that we can’t confirm go back to the writer or come out. Every piece lists how many facts were checked and how many came out.

How each statement is handled

What the piece saysExampleWhat we do
Opinion“The council should have waited for public input.”Left as written. The label on every piece says the views are the writer’s.
A fact we can confirm“The commission approved it 2–1.”Kept, with a link to the record.
An unconfirmed fact about a named person or group“The developer paid the commissioner.”Sent back for a source, or removed. Never published with a “couldn’t find it” note.
An unconfirmed observation that isn’t about anyone’s conduct“Traffic on my street has doubled.”Kept only if framed as the writer’s own observation (“I’ve seen…”); otherwise removed.
A fact the records contradictA wrong vote count or dateSent back to fix. If it isn’t fixed, the piece doesn’t run.

What counts as a record

  • Government records posted publicly: meeting agendas, minutes and recordings; budgets; bills, compare documents and vote pages; disclosure and campaign finance filings; business registrations; court dockets
  • News reports, cited by name and date (for what the report says, not as proof beyond it)
  • The group’s or person’s own public statements and websites

We don’t file records requests to check a piece; if the record isn’t already public, the statement is treated as unconfirmed.

What every published piece shows

  • Letter or essay, writer’s name and city
  • Published date, and an updated date if anything changed
  • How many factual statements were checked, and how many were removed or sent back
  • Links to the records used
  • Any tie the writer disclosed to a group involved in the subject
  • A change log at the bottom listing every correction, with its date

Corrections

If a published piece turns out to contain an error, it’s corrected in place, the updated date changes, and the change log says what changed and when. Nothing is quietly edited.
